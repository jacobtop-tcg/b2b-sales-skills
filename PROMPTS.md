# Prompt pack

Short prompts for ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or any assistant that does not read skill folders. Paste one as your first message, then answer its questions. For the full procedure, paste the matching `SKILL.md` instead. Each prompt also has its own page with a copy button: https://topmoller.com/tools/ai-sales-skills/

Every prompt keeps the same rules: you write first and the assistant challenges; there are no scores; "confirmed" means the right person at the customer agreed to a playback or recap, or acted; the assistant never moves a stage or a forecast category; anything not discussed stays empty.

## Pre-call plan

```
Prepare me for a first meeting with [company] ([website]). I am meeting [name, role]. We sell [one line]. Our customer story: [one customer like them, one number before and after]. Use public sources only, and give the link for every fact.
1. The company in two lines, from its own about page.
2. What happened in the last six months: news, an interview with one of their leaders, and what their latest annual report says about [our words].
3. The person I meet: role, time in it, promotions, results they have shared, and the roles around them. Mark what suggests power and influence as a guess.
4. What connects us: past customers, shared contacts, places or groups.
5. For why change, why now and why us: what the research suggests, with sources. Then ask me to write my own 3 whys, and wait.
Then draft three questions that test my 3 whys, with who asks each (the Sales Engineer asks what the problem costs today), and a 30-second opening built on our customer story. End with what the meeting should leave us with: the problem in their words, whether they could be a champion, and a next step with a date. Invent nothing.
```

## Before a meeting

```
Help me prepare a customer meeting. Before you suggest anything, ask me to write my hypothesis as the 3 whys, in the customer's words: why change (the problem, and what doing nothing costs), why now (the date or event) and why us (why our way fits better than the alternatives, doing nothing included). Then challenge it with the facts I give you, and only those facts. Show what we know about how they decide, on MEDDPICC, in their words with who said it, apart from what we are guessing. No scores. Give me open questions that test the guesses, and say who asks each; the sales engineer asks what the problem costs today. End with what the meeting should leave us with: the problem in their words, a potential champion and an agreed next step with a date.
```

## After a call

```
Help us debrief this customer meeting as a team. First ask us what went well and what we would do differently, and write our answers down. Then, from the notes below, write three things we did well and three we could do better, each tied to a moment in the meeting; three things we learned about the customer's buying decision, in their own words with who said them and the part of MEDDICC each sheds light on; and three things we still need to get in place, with who could tell us and how we find out next time. No scores. Write only what the notes show. [paste notes]
```

## The recap mail

```
First ask me, in two or three lines, what I heard as the customer's main problem and what it costs. Then draft a "what we heard" mail to the customer from these notes, in their words, at most three bullets per section: Background, Challenges (with what they cost), Positive business outcomes, Minimum required capabilities, Reverse timeline (planned back from their compelling event). Open with the agreed next step. Mark anything the notes do not support as [check]. No product pitch. I will edit and send it myself. [paste notes]
```

## Deal review

```
Review this deal with me as my leader would in our 1:1. First ask me to tell the deal from the customer's side: who it is with, and why change, why now and why us in their words. Write my answer down. Then show what we know in the customer's words (who said it, when) apart from what is only relayed or assumed, what the customer has done (with dates) apart from what they only promised, and who has acted for us (a champion) and who only gave advice (a coach). Go through MEDDPICC as questions. No scores. Ask me the two or three questions that help me think, and wait for my answers. End with what the next conversation must find out and who at the customer could tell us. Then ask me for the next move. I decide it.
```

## Forecast category

```
Suggest a forecast category for this deal. First ask me which category I would call and why. Then write what the customer has said and done about pain, champion, economic buyer and paper process, each as assumed, relayed or confirmed, with the moment behind it. No numbers. Confirmed means the right person agreed to a playback or recap, or acted; a champion is confirmed only by an action, and a promise is not one. A visible opportunity needs pain the customer has confirmed and a potential champion, relayed or confirmed. Commit (will close this quarter) needs the economic buyer, the paper process and a champion who has acted, all confirmed by the customer. Upside (may close this quarter) needs a champion who has acted. Future will not close this quarter. Black hole: qualified out or no move in 30 days. The stage sets the highest category (01 none, 02 and 03 future, 04 and 05 upside, 06 commit, 07 won) and the evidence can only lower it. Tell me what evidence would move it up. I decide.
```

## Champion or coach

```
Help me test whether my contact is a champion or a coach. First ask me what they have done for us since our last review and what I think their personal win is, and write my answer down. Then list what they did for us since our last review, each with its proof, and keep promises in a separate list: a promise to act is not an action. Check power, influence and their personal win. Then suggest one small ask with a date that only a champion could do, such as a meeting with the economic buyer. I decide what they are.
```

## QBR

```
Prepare a QBR for this account. First ask me how the account stands and what worries me most, and write my answer down. Use only numbers the customer measured or confirmed. Open with their value pyramid and what changed, then last quarter (what we agreed, numbers beaten and missed), then next quarter: new goals the customer names, what missing each would cost, and how we measure them. Check the champion is still in role and acting. Keep support cases and feature requests out of the QBR. Flag if the renewal is within two quarters.
```

## Value framework refresh

```
Refresh our value framework from this quarter's customer calls and QBR notes. First ask me which drivers I think moved this quarter. Per driver, collect the customers' exact words with source and whether we may use them, and results only where the customer confirmed the number. List reasons customers gave that fit no driver, and drivers nobody mentioned. Propose at most three changes with their evidence. The leadership team decides.
```

## Practise with a buyer

```
Play the buyer in a [discovery call / new business meeting] so I can practise. Here is the brief: [the pain in numbers, what they need, objections]. Stay in role, never try to trip me up, reveal the pain only to good questions, and push back if I talk about the product too early. When I say stop, first ask what I would do differently, then give feedback on: opens with their challenges; open, two-sided questions; needs in their words; numbers on the pain; proof that fits; plays back and gets agreement; questions that lead to our strengths; a next step with a date. Then two things you liked and two I could do differently.
```
