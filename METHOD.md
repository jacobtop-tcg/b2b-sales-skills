# The method on one page

Every skill in this pack runs on the same few rules. They are here once, so you can read them before you install anything.

## One idea

MEDDPICC is the team's shared language about how a customer decides. Its elements are there every time a customer makes a decision, whether you know it or not. Make it your job to know: what we understand in the customer's words, where we are guessing, who acted and who only advised, and what the next conversation has to find out. There are no scores. The question that tests it is: how do you know?

## Know apart from guess

Write what you know at its real strength, in words:

- **Assumed:** our own belief. Nothing from the customer.
- **Relayed:** one person told us. Not tested.
- **Confirmed:** the right person agreed to a playback or recap, or acted. Agreeing to prepare a playback is not a test.

Quote the customer's own words with who said them and when. What the customer did, with its date, stays apart from what they promised. Anything not discussed stays empty.

## Champion or coach

The champion is in the ring and takes the blows. The coach stands on the sideline and gives advice. Be sceptical: call a helpful contact a potential champion until they act for us. A promise to act, or an answer on a call, is not an action.

## The eight parts (MEDDPICC)

The pain comes first, and only the customer can say it is a problem for them.

| Part | What it is | The question |
|---|---|---|
| Identify pain | The business need that makes them act. | Who at the customer says it is a problem, and what does it cost them? |
| Metrics | How success is measured, in numbers. | Which numbers, today and target? |
| Champion | Sells for you inside the customer. | What have they done for us since the last review? |
| Economic buyer | Owns the budget and the final yes. | Have we met them? Do they agree with the case? |
| Decision criteria | What they will judge us on, in their words. | Which of our differences are among them? |
| Decision process | The steps, people and timing to a decision. | Who is involved at each step, and when? |
| Paper process | How the contract gets signed. | Who signs, which reviews, how long, and any term outside our standard? |
| Competition | Who or what else wants the budget, including doing nothing. | Why would they choose us, and why not? |

## The 3 whys

Why change? Why now? Why us? In the customer's words. If the seller cannot tell them that way, the deal is back in discovery.

## Stages and their exit

A deal moves when the customer has done something, never when we have. Talk each exit through as a question about the deal.

| Stage | It moves on when |
|---|---|
| 01 Leads and first meeting | The customer confirms the pain. |
| 02 Discovery and analysis | After the new business meeting, the champion has done something for us: booked the economic buyer, or brought in people of power. |
| 03 Solution design | The champion names us the vendor of choice and can defend the 3 whys. |
| 04 Executive alignment | The economic buyer sponsors the next step, and the playback is booked. |
| 05 Validation | The economic buyer and the champion confirm the business case. |
| 06 Proposal and negotiation | Executive sign-off and signature. |
| 07 Implement, launch and adopt | The customer confirms the first result from the case. |

## Forecast categories

A deal enters the forecast as a **visible opportunity**: pain the customer has confirmed, and a potential champion, relayed or confirmed. Under that, it is not a deal yet.

| Category | Means | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Commit | Will close this quarter. | The economic buyer, the paper process and a champion who has acted, all confirmed by the customer. |
| Upside | May close this quarter. | A champion who has acted. |
| Future | Will not close this quarter. | A visible opportunity. |
| Black hole | Qualified out, or no move in 30 days. | Nothing. Qualifying a deal out is a win. |

The stage sets the highest category a deal can have: stages 01 none, 02 and 03 future, 04 and 05 upside, 06 commit, 07 won. The evidence can only lower it.

## Bring it in slowly

A team learns this by using it every day. Start speaking the language on live deals, with two skills, and have extreme patience with the people who are learning it.

## The rules for AI

1. AI drafts. People decide.
2. The people who did the work write first, then AI adds and challenges. Never the other way round.
3. "Confirmed" belongs to the customer. The seller signs off a draft; that does not confirm anything.
4. Relayed or confirmed needs a customer moment: a call moment, their mail, or their reply to "what we heard". Without one, it is assumed.
5. No scores, totals or rankings of the letters.
6. AI never moves a stage or a forecast category. It says what evidence would.
7. "Not discussed" stays empty. A fact without a source is marked unverified.
8. No judgements of feelings, tone or confidence on people.
9. Every message that goes out under a person's name is read and sent by that person.
10. Ask before you record, offer to stop, and keep recordings only as long as you need them.
