# QBR prep

A QBR that opens with "how's it going?" brings back support cases and feature requests. Open with the customer's value pyramid instead, and move the goal post every quarter.

## Rules you keep

- Start from what the customer bought: the one-page case, the proof of value document, the champion plan and what we promised at the handover.
- Numbers count only when the customer measured or confirmed them. Mark the rest [check]. Do not work out new figures from their numbers, such as a money value or a return against our price. Leave the field empty with [ask: the question for the customer]. That goes for the cost of missing a goal and the renewal case too.
- Where the inputs are silent, for example on the economic buyer or whether a renewal case exists, write "not in the inputs [ask]". Never fill the gap with a guess such as none, late or overdue. Put quotation marks only around words the inputs quote.
- When the CSM says how someone feels ("they love us"), do not pass it on as that person's view. Ask what they did for us, and when.
- A goal without the cost of missing it is a wish. Every new goal states what missing it would cost.
- Support cases go to the service review support already runs. Feature requests go to product, qualified like a deal. A feature request never becomes an upsell.

## Steps

1. **Inputs, and the CSM's read first.** Ask for the case, last quarter's QBR notes, the agreed goals and any usage or result numbers. Then ask the CSM how the account stands and which two parts are weakest. Write their answer down in their words before you give any view.
2. **Their value pyramid.** What has changed: new initiatives, new challenges, new people?
3. **Last quarter.** What we agreed, and the numbers we beat and missed. Report each miss as the notes give it, and never set a new deadline for it. A goal missed and still behind plan counts as missed twice.
4. **Next quarter.** Two or three new goals the customer names, what missing each would cost, and how it is measured.
5. **Stories and numbers.** A story or reference we may use, with permission, and the numbers behind it.
6. **The champion.** Same role, same power and influence? A champion gone, a goal missed twice, or the case they bought behind its date is a renewal risk. Suggest it for the team's forecast with the customer evidence that would clear it; the CSM and their leader decide. If the champion left, find where they went, since that may be a new lead, and ask the successor what they used before, since a competitor may come back.
7. **Who is in the room.** The champion every time; the economic buyer before the renewal.
8. **Renewal and upsell.** Two quarters before the renewal, write the renewal case, bring the economic buyer, and check in the contract who signs and the notice date. An upsell is its own deal, with its own stages and letters. Open it only from a goal the customer named, for something we already sell, and not while the renewal is at risk.
9. **Requests.** For each feature request: is there a champion, what is the case, what happens if we don't deliver, and why this one first? Answer only with what the customer said or did, otherwise [ask]. Asking for it is not an action, so the requester is not yet a champion. A request with an [ask] left is not yet qualified.

## Output

```
ACCOUNT: [customer]   QBR: [date]   RENEWAL: [date]
CSM READ (before ours): [their words]
1 THEIR VALUE PYRAMID: [what changed]
2 LAST QUARTER: agreed [...], beat [...], missed [...]
3 NEXT QUARTER: goal / cost of missing it / how we measure  (2 or 3 rows)
4 STORY AND NUMBERS: [...] permission: yes / ask
CHAMPION: [still in role? acted since last QBR?]
RISK: none / suggested: [why], clears when [customer evidence]
IN THE ROOM: [...]
RENEWAL CASE DUE: [date]   SIGNER AND NOTICE DATE: [...]
UPSELL TO OPEN AS A DEAL: [...]
TO SERVICE REVIEW: [...]   TO PRODUCT: qualified [...] / not yet [...]
```
