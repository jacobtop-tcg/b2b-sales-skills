# Pre-call plan

Do the searching so the seller can do the thinking. Every fact has a source, and every guess says it is one.

## Rules you keep

- Use public sources only, and give the link for every fact. If you cannot search the web, say so, and work only from what the seller gives you.
- About people, use only what they share in their working life: role, time in it, promotions, results they talk about. Nothing private.
- Mark every inference as a guess. Look for evidence of power, influence and a personal win. A champion also acts for us.
- The seller writes the 3 whys. You lay out what the research suggests for each; they decide. The 3 whys stay a hypothesis until the customer has said them.
- Never invent a name, a number, a quote or a customer story. If the seller has no story with a number, the opening says what we do, without one.
- No scores. You never contact anyone.

## Steps

1. **The meeting.** Ask: which company, its website, who the seller meets (name and role), what we sell in one line, and one customer like them with a number from before and after. Wait for the answers.
2. **The company.** Two lines from its own about page and LinkedIn page: what they do and where they are going.
3. **What is happening.** News from the last six months, an interview with one of their leaders, and what their latest annual report says about the seller's words (for example automation or compliance). Each with its link and date.
4. **The people.** The person the seller meets: role, how long in it, promotions, results they share. The roles around them who could own the problem or the budget. Mark what suggests power and influence as a guess.
5. **Connect the dots.** Past customers of ours they worked at, shared contacts, schools, cities or groups, or a competitor of a customer we can name.
6. **The 3 whys.** For why change, why now and why us: what the research suggests, with its source. Then ask the seller to write their own 3 whys, and wait.
7. **Questions that test it.** Three open questions aimed at the biggest guesses, each with who asks. The Sales Engineer asks what the problem costs today.
8. **The opening.** Thirty seconds: the customer like them and their number, the link to this company's situation, and a small ask for a few minutes.
9. **Leave with.** The problem in their words, whether this person could be a champion, and a next step with a date.

## Output

```
PRE-CALL PLAN: [company], [person, role], [date of the meeting]

THE COMPANY: [two lines] (source)
WHAT IS HAPPENING: [news, interview, annual report] (source, date)
OUR STORY: [customer like them, one number before and after] (the seller's)
THE PEOPLE: [person: role, time in it, promotions, results] (source)
  Around them: [roles, and what suggests power and influence: a guess]
CONNECT THE DOTS: [what we have in common] (source)
WHAT THE RESEARCH SUGGESTS: why change ... / why now ... / why us ... (sources)
THE 3 WHYS (the seller's hypothesis): why change ... / why now ... / why us ...
QUESTIONS THAT TEST IT: 1. ... (asks: ...) 2. ... 3. ...
OPENING (30 seconds): ...
LEAVE WITH: the problem in their words / could they be a champion / next step [what, who, date]
TO CHECK BEFORE THE MEETING: [each inference, and any source older than six months]
```

Close with one line: test the 3 whys with meeting-prep before the meeting.
