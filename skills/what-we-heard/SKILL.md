# What we heard

The recap is the cheapest way to move a letter from relayed to confirmed. If the customer corrects it, you learn something. If they agree, the evidence gets stronger.

## Rules you keep

- Use the customer's words from the notes, and only what the seller gave you. Do not improve them into marketing language. A document you have not seen is named, not described.
- Write each commitment as it was said, by the person who said it. A promise stays a promise, not work under way.
- At most three bullets per section.
- A claim the notes do not fully support is marked [check] for the seller. A section that was not discussed gets one plain question to the customer instead (a target, a cost, a date, who signs), never a prompt for the seller to fill in with their own view.
- No product pitch. The mail is about them. Leave competitors, price and discounts out; tell the seller in one line what they mean for the next step.
- Leave stage and forecast out. A reply to the recap can confirm letters; it does not move a stage by itself.
- You draft. The seller reads, edits and sends it from their own mailbox.

## Steps

1. Ask for the notes or the debrief, the names on the invite, and the next step that was agreed. Ask the seller to say first, in two or three lines, what they heard as the customer's main problem and what it costs. Write that down as theirs, then draft, and say where the notes differ. A transcript alone does not replace it.
2. Fill the template below.
3. Point out which sections were thin, so the seller knows what to ask about next time. Keep everything outside the mail to a few short lines, and do not explain the evidence ladder.

## The mail

```
Subject: What we heard, [date]

Dear [names],

Thank you for your time today. As agreed, our next step is [step, who and when. If no date was agreed, add a question that proposes one].

To make sure I heard you correctly, here is a short recap. If anything is off or missing, please tell me.

Background:
- [the situation and the initiatives this relates to]

Challenges:
- [the challenges and what they cost the business]

Positive business outcomes:
- [what good looks like for them; we will spend more time here next time]

Minimum required capabilities:
- [what any solution must do]

Reverse timeline:
- [the milestones, planned back from (their compelling event)]

Best regards,
[name]
```

Send it as a reply to the meeting invite, the same day. If it goes out later, replace "today", "tomorrow" and "next week" with real dates counted from the meeting day, also in what the customer promised.
