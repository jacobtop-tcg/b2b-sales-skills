# Deal review

Show where the deal really stands and where the effort goes next. The ground rule in the room: it is more important to get it right than to be right.

## Rules you keep

- Score each letter on the evidence ladder: 0 not asked, 1 assumed (our belief), 2 relayed (heard from one person, not tested), 3 confirmed (by the right person, and tested). For the champion, 3 means they acted for us; a promise to act, or an answer on a call, is not an action. Tested means they agreed to a playback or recap, or acted; agreeing to prepare a playback is not a test.
- Give each letter one score, set by its weakest part, and name the stronger parts in the moment column. A yes with an open condition is not a 3; a letter at 3 is never listed as missing.
- "Not discussed" stays "Not discussed" and scores 0. A number the customer did not give stays empty, and you never work one out from theirs; if two of their numbers clash, quote both with who said them and when, and ask which holds. A goal date is not a decision or signing date. Keep the customer's tense and strength when you paraphrase, in every row and section: a plan they announced ('we will ask') stays a plan, never 'they have asked'. Under Competition, write only what the customer said or did; your reading of a risk goes under the questions.
- No judgements about anyone's feelings, tone or confidence. Only what was said and done.
- "Show me the moment": every 2 or 3 needs the customer moment behind it, a call moment, their mail, or their reply to "what we heard". No moment, no score above 1.
- Never add the scores up. Look at the two weakest letters.
- A visible opportunity needs pain at 3 and a potential champion at 2 or more. Under that, say "not a deal yet". Qualifying a deal out is a good outcome; say so when the evidence points there.
- If the seller answers "I think", the letter is assumed: 1 at most.
- The seller and their leader decide. You ask and show, and never move a stage or forecast category. If the seller names a forecast category, say which letters do not support it and leave the full check to forecast-category.

## Steps

1. **The owner tells the deal** (5 minutes in a live review). First ask which two letters the seller thinks are weakest, and why, and write the answer down before you score anything. Then: who it is with, the 3 whys in the customer's words, what is done and what is next. If anything is missing, ask once more and wait; never fill it in from the record. If they cannot tell the 3 whys that way, say which why is missing and what it risks now; the evidence points back to discovery.
2. **Score the eight letters** with the moment behind each score.
3. **The two weakest letters.** Compare them with the seller's own pick. If more than two tie, take first those in the current stage's exit criterion (01 the customer confirms the pain; 02 after the new business meeting, the champion books the economic buyer or brings in people of power; 03 the champion names us the vendor of choice and can defend the 3 whys; 04 the economic buyer sponsors the next step and the playback is booked; 05 the economic buyer and the champion confirm the business case; 06 executive sign-off and signature); if that does not settle it, list the tie. Ask the questions below and wait for the seller's answers; a question only the customer can answer goes into the actions.
4. **On any deal**, ask: How do you know? What would make them say no? What did the customer say, in their own words?
5. **Actions:** what, who and when. Suggest that the leader takes one of them.

## Questions, letter by letter

- **Identify pain:** What happens if they do nothing? Is it big, measurable and urgent? Who feels it?
- **Metrics:** Is the case in numbers, and does the economic buyer agree with it?
- **Champion:** How did we test them, and what did they do? What have we given them to win with?
- **Economic buyer:** Who is it, and have we met them?
- **Decision criteria:** Has the economic buyer agreed the criteria, and who has joined since? Which of our differences are among them?
- **Decision process:** What are the steps, the people and the time for each step?
- **Paper process:** Who signs, and what has to happen before they sign? Will procurement ask for other bids?
- **Competition:** Which of our differences have we built into their criteria? What would make them choose someone else, or nothing?
