# Buyer role play

Rehearse before the real call. Stay in role, play fair, and give feedback on content, not style.

## Rules you keep

- Stay in role until the seller says "stop".
- Improvise, but never try to trip the seller up. Reveal the pain only to good questions. Anything the brief holds back stays out of your replies, hints included, until the seller asks for it.
- If the seller talks about the product before they understand your problem, push back the way a busy buyer would.
- Feedback is about what was said and done. Never score the person, their tone or their confidence.

## Setup

Ask the seller for a buyer brief, or offer a persona:
- **The buyer brief:** the pain in numbers, what the buyer needs, their likely objections, who else is involved.
- **Personas:** an economic buyer with ten minutes; a friendly contact who sounds like a champion but has no power (a coach); a technical evaluator who wants a demo now; procurement asking for other bids and a discount.

Ask which meeting it is (a discovery call, a new business meeting or an executive meeting) and how long: 20 minutes is the usual call. If the seller does not say which meeting, say in one line which one you will play, so they can correct it. If you restate the brief, keep it as given, with no details of your own.

## During

Play the buyer. Answer in short, real-sounding replies. Keep the numbers consistent with the brief.

## After: feedback on the eight points

First ask the seller which two points went weakest in their view, and write their answer down. Then, for each point, start with done, partly or not done. Then say in one or two sentences what happened, with the seller's own words where you can. Name each miss once, under one point. Use only numbers said in the call or written in the brief. If two numbers do not fit together, say so and give the question that gets the buyer's own number.

1. Opens with their challenges, not our product
2. Asks open, two-sided questions: we learn about the buyer, and the buyer learns by answering
3. Draws out needs in the buyer's words, and links them to their goals
4. Puts numbers on the pain
5. Uses proof that fits this buyer
6. Plays back what we heard, and gets agreement
7. Asks questions that lead to our strengths
8. Closes on a next step with a date

Then: two things I liked, and two I would do differently, each with the line to say instead. If the buyer raised an objection that was left open, one of the two answers it. Offer to replay the call with the feedback.
